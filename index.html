01 — About

Business context, analytical direction.

Commerce and analytics training, applied inside a fast-moving event production business.

My background combines a BCom in Accounting and Commerce with an MBA in Business Analytics. That gives me a practical view of how money, approvals and reporting actually move through a company.

I start every piece of work with the people who do it: discovery interviews and workshops, a clear AS-IS and TO-BE process, then requirements a team can build and test.

At an AV and event production company in Dubai, I turned that analysis into 10 internal tools in 5 months for finance, operations, sales and marketing. They replaced paper logbooks, handwritten claims and hour-long bill checks, and they're used every day.

Understand the work, map it honestly, then automate only what the evidence says will save time.

03 — Analysis process

How I solve business problems

A repeatable path from a messy manual process to a tool people actually use.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Interview the people doing the work and watch the process run with real documents.

    →
  2. 02

    Map AS-IS

    Document every step, hand-off, approval and delay exactly as it happens today.

    →
  3. 03

    Design TO-BE

    Find the gaps, agree the better process and the success measures with stakeholders.

    →
  4. 04

    Specify

    Write requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria the team can test against.

    →
  5. 05

    Build

    Deliver in small, testable pieces with the data source the team already trusts.

    →
  6. 06

    Test

    Run test cases, pilot testing and UAT with real users and real files.

    →
  7. 07

    Train

    Visual guides and walkthroughs so the tool is adopted, not just shipped.

    →
  8. 08

    Measure

    Track time saved and errors avoided, then improve the next version.

    →

08 — Principles

Principles behind the work

The rules I come back to on every project.

1

People before tools.

The best requirements come from watching the work, not guessing it.

2

AS-IS before TO-BE.

A process has to be understood honestly before it can be improved.

3

Automate what saves real time.

If a step doesn't cost the team time or money, it doesn't need a tool.

4

Adoption is part of delivery.

A tool nobody uses is unfinished. Pilots, training and guides are part of the job.
